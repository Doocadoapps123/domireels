# How Domi makes a Reel

What the person who directed Domi's best Reels by hand knew, written down so Muse can direct them alone.
Every claim here comes from a Reel we made and what the restaurant owner said about it.

## 1. What we are making

- A Reel, not an ad. It looks like someone filmed it on a phone in a working restaurant: close, a little handheld,
  the real sound of the kitchen, text typed in the app. Not a studio product shot, not a slow push on a still dish,
  not a commercial edited to one big final hit. Owner, 3 Nov: "eso no es un reel, es una animación... parecen
  comerciales y no reels".
- 9 to 13 seconds. A 6-7 s Reel reads as a short ad. Owner on a 6.8 s Reel: "muy corto, parece un comercial corto en
  lugar de un reel para subir a Instagram y TikTok".
- One small story where something happens to the food: it gets built, poured, pressed, dipped, torn, pulled,
  revealed. A dish sitting there, or photos passing by, is not a Reel. Owner on a Reel of stock flames and the real
  photo: "apenas usó fotos, ni siquiera movimiento".
- The food is always this restaurant's real food, exactly as its photo and its owner say: the same cut, the same
  cheese (slices are not shredded), the same bread, the same portion. The real photo is the truth; the owner's note
  on the dish beats the photo.

## 2. Our camera: what each tool really does

**Peel and rebuild.** An image editor takes the real photo apart from the top: the last sauce off, then the next,
then the cheese, the meat, down to the empty container. A build shot is a 4 s video that starts on one of those
frames and ends on the next one up. The video model is given both ends, so it only has to animate a hand adding a
step it can already see. This made our best Reels: the yaroa (fries poured, pork piled, cheese laid, three sauces
drawn one by one) and the chicharrón (fries and chunks poured into the tray). It works for anything built in visible
layers: loaded fries, yaroas, nachos, bowls, platters, plated sides, pizza toppings. It cannot open closed food: the
inside of a burrito, a sandwich or an empanada cannot be peeled.

**Hands on the real dish.** A 4 s video that starts on the real photo (often framed tight on one part of it). A hand
or a fork acts on what is already on the plate: lifts a piece, dips it in the sauce that is there, squeezes the lime
that is there, tears it open, pulls the cheese, presses the bun. Our best payoffs are these. Food is held the way
people hold it: a folded taco cradled from below or pinched across the fold, never lifted by one edge of the tortilla;
a birria taco is dipped whole, nobody pulls the meat out with a fork.

**Equipment.** The restaurant's own photos of its fryer, plancha, grill or oven first; otherwise free real footage
that belongs to this restaurant only. A real equipment frame can get this dish swapped in (the chicharrón inside a real
fryer basket, lifted out dripping oil). The equipment must be how this dish is really cooked: fried food goes in the
fryer; a ceviche never sees a grill; flames in front of a dish that comes in sauce promise another dish.

**Edit tricks.** A hard cut costs nothing and always looks real. Magic, surprises and reveals are made in the edit:
an empty container or table (the "empty" layer) and a hand that snaps, knocks, wipes the lens or lowers a lid, then a
hard cut on the beat to the real dish. The video model never makes food appear by itself; every Reel that asked it to
(a lime rain that fills plates, a box that fills by itself) produced nothing usable and burned its budget.

**The real photo as a flash.** 0.4-0.6 s of the real photo, or a tight crop of its best detail, between two video
shots. Real pixels between generated ones make the whole Reel read as real.

**Wrapping.** The editor can also add to the real photo: the burrito wrapped in foil, the box closed, a lid on the
bowl. A shot that starts wrapped and ends on the real photo is hands unwrapping it.

**A place.** The editor can move the real dish onto a restaurant table or counter when its photo was taken somewhere
else. Every Reel looks like it happens at the restaurant: never a living room, a desk, a rainy window or a car (Pikalo
on a rainy night, 4 Nov: "prefiero contenido que siempre parezca que está en el restaurante"; Santia's empanada on an
office desk with a laptop: "devastador"). A moment of the week lives in the words, not in a fake place.

**Text on screen.** Typed like a creator does it in the app: a hook line in the first second, at most two more. It
talks like a person ("POV: pediste la yaroa de pierna", "3 salsas, 0 arrepentimientos"), never like an ad slogan.
Only true facts: the dish name, the city, what is really in it. Never a price or an offer we were not given.

**Music and sound.** ElevenLabs makes the music. Each video shot carries its own kitchen sound (the sizzle, the pour,
the crunch), and it stays on top.

What the video model does badly: food that is in neither its first nor its last frame (it comes out pale, puffy, cut
wrong), people in motion and faces (they read as AI: a person eating, walking or talking never goes on camera; the house
cast appears only holding or showing the dish, the box or the bag, almost still), liquids that must appear from
nothing, keeping count (three tacos become two), text and logos. And hands that handle a dish the way nobody does:
a sandwich, a taco or an empanada is held in the hand, never lifted with a fork (Pikalo, 4 Nov).

**Every shot does something.** A hand or a fork that hovers, touches or lifts "a little" is not a shot: the action
finishes where the camera sees it (the yolk breaks and runs, the bite lifts to the lens, the cheese stretches, the
sauce drips). A dish just sitting there is a flash at most. Bohío's mangú, 4 Nov: "la mayoría de los cortes son el
tenedor queriendo hacer algo y no hace nada".

**Several dishes together.** The real dishes, cut out of their own photos, set side by side on one table: a video
where only the camera moves across them, like a documentary, and the food stays still. Then any dish can get its own
shot from its own photo with its own motion. One video shot never has to move several dishes at once.

## 3. How we shoot

- Every video shot lasts 4 s and we keep only its good seconds. A take whose first second is perfect and whose last
  second melts still gives a clean 1 s cut. So plan 6-8 video shots: the edit needs 14-20 good pieces.
- Every change has a visible cause: a hand, a tool, a pour from something already in frame.
- Anyone in a shot is there from its first frame or enters from the edge. A new setup comes after a hard cut.
- The camera stays close and still, a little handheld; the food and the hands move. No slow pans, no orbits.
- Kitchen order: the finished dish never shows up before its build ends.
- Generous: full portions, a real squeeze with a visible spurt and drip, cheese that stretches long. A token drop
  does not make anyone hungry.

## 4. Rhythm, length, music

Two rhythms. The idea picks one.
- Fast cut: pieces of 0.4-0.8 s, 14-20 cuts, and a 1.5-2.5 s hold on the payoff. Owner: "mientras menos duren los
  cortes menos tiempo hay de pensar en AI". Builds, tours and magic.
- Long takes: 3-5 pieces of 1.5-3 s where one continuous action plays out (a dip, a pull, a press, a melt), with the
  food's own sound up front. Juicy proof and melt stares.

Music fits the food and the mood of the idea, and is not what another restaurant got this week. It is the bed of a
Reel, not an ad jingle: not every track is "full energy from the first beat to one big final hit". Some Reels have no
music at all, only the kitchen sound and text. Owner on two Reels the same day: "hizo el 7 y el 8 idénticos, misma
música, misma parrilla, eso está muy mal". Merengue típico worked for the chicharrón; dembow for the yaroa; a
merengue de orquesta did not ("esa música no es vacana... si es comida fast food algo más joven").
Each genre played the way it is really played, at its own tempo and with its own instruments. The dembow the owner
called "excelente" (Yaroa de Pierna): Dominican dembow at 130 bpm, heavy dembow kick, güira, bright synth stabs. A
dembow at 104 bpm with a synth-brass lead on top: "la música está rara". A mellow afrobeats bed at 92 bpm under a
melt: "esa música no va, más movida".

## 5. Reels we made, and what the owner said

Learn why each one worked or failed. Never reuse a structure from here: every Reel is its own idea (Yansel: "no es
copiar nada más, es tener la creatividad de crear cosas así que nadie nunca haya hecho"). Most of the good ones end
on a cheese stretch; that is what those dishes had, not how a Reel must end.

Good:
1. Yaroa de Pierna, Master Pizza Tropical (directed by hand, 10 s, 16 cuts). Hook: a tight crop of the cheese sauce
   stream, 0.6 s. A real fryer basket in boiling oil, 0.5 + 0.4 s. Fries poured into the foil pan (empty to fries),
   0.5 + 0.6. Tongs pile the pork (fries to meat), 0.6 + 0.5. Cheese slices laid on, 0.4 + 0.4. Ketchup lines and a
   punch-in, mayonnaise, cheese sauce. A flash of the real photo of the sauces; a flash of the sister Yaroa de Pollo.
   Payoff: a fork lifts a bite and the mozzarella stretches, held 2.4 s. Dembow. Owner: "muy real"; "la yaroa
   contiene queso mozzarella que se estira, eso quedó perfecto".
2. Chicharrón de Cerdo, Master Pizza Tropical (by hand, 11 s, 17 cuts). A real fryer; the restaurant's chicharrón
   inside a real fryer basket lifted out dripping oil; fries poured into the tray; the chunks poured next to them; a
   flash of the real tray; a lime squeezed hard over the chunks; a flash of the chunks; two hands break a chunk in
   half, held 1.6 s. Merengue típico. Owner: "muy real la verdad"; "vamos bien verdaderamente... quedó súper".
3. Torta de birria, Dora Taquería (8 s). A 4 s plancha blitz from meat on the plancha with five cuts inside it (meat
   dropped, chopped, cheese laid on, onto the roll, sauce), then a hard cut to the real torta pressed by gloved hands
   until it oozes. Owner: "muy bueno". The one miss: it showed shredded cheese where the torta has slices.
4. Consomé que abraza, Dora Taquería (by Muse, 7 s). Two flashes of the real tacos and the consomé cup; a hand lifts a
   taco, dips it in the consomé, lifts it dripping; tears it open over the cup with the Monterey Jack stretching,
   held 2.7 s. The owner chose that cheese ending.
5. Pikalo clásico, Pikalo (by Muse, 10 s, 16 cuts). A fryer; the pressed sandwich halves set on the plate, pressed
   until the cheese oozes; fries poured beside them; a hand lifts a half with the cheese stretching.
6. Yaroa de Pierna, Master Pizza Tropical (by Muse, 10 s, 17 cuts). Like the first but its own: the yaroa built
   sauce by sauce with cuts to the sister yaroas between the sauces. Owner: "excelente"; "me gusta que hizo
   creatividad en la yaroa, que no fue a lo mismo de armándola, sino que iba haciendo diferentes cortes a medida que
   iba poniendo una salsa".

Bad:
- "Fuego de la parrilla" (Balis) and "Se vacía la fajita" (Dora), the same day: stock grill flames, a flash and the
  real photo, 5-6 s. "Horrible, apenas usó fotos ni siquiera movimiento"; "hizo el 7 y el 8 idénticos, misma música,
  misma parrilla".
- "El burrito que respira" (Dora), 6.8 s of a burrito sitting there: "muy corto, parece un comercial corto".
- "Parrillada por magia" (Master Pizza) and the boba of The Purple Bear: the magic was asked of the video model and
  never happened; a cup standing still on a split background; the same trap track as everything else. "Eso no es un
  reel, es una animación... está empezando a usar la misma música... parecen comerciales".
- "Ceviche frente a la serie" (Balis): the place never appeared, so the idea was gone.
